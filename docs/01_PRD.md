# Vision

Many people struggle to achieve long-term goals not because they lack motivation, but because they find it difficult to translate those goals into realistic daily actions.

When planning becomes overwhelming, people tend to delay, rely on short-term bursts of effort, or eventually stop planning altogether. Over time, daily tasks become disconnected from long-term aspirations, and planning itself loses its value.

Daily Routine Helper exists to bridge this gap.

It helps users transform meaningful long-term goals into achievable daily progress, while continuously adapting to their execution, reflection, and changing circumstances. Rather than generating static to-do lists, it provides a dynamic planning experience that evolves with the user.

The purpose of this product is not to maximize productivity or encourage users to complete more tasks. Instead, it aims to help users build confidence in long-term growth by showing that consistent, achievable daily progress can lead to meaningful outcomes.

Ultimately, Daily Routine Helper is designed to become a trusted planning companion—one that reduces the friction of planning, supports sustainable habits, and helps users believe that lasting progress is built one day at a time.

# Problem Statement

Many people have meaningful long-term goals, such as preparing for a new career, improving their health, or learning a new skill. However, turning those ambitions into realistic daily actions is often far more difficult than expected.

Most people do not fail because they lack motivation or discipline. Instead, they struggle to answer a much simpler question every day:

**"What should I do today?"**

Existing planning tools primarily focus on organizing tasks that users have already decided to do. They assume that users know how to plan, how to prioritize, and how to translate long-term goals into daily actions.

In reality, many users struggle before they even begin.

When planning becomes overwhelming, people often create plans that are either too ambitious or disconnected from real-world constraints. As those plans repeatedly fail, confidence gradually decreases. Users begin to associate planning with frustration instead of progress, eventually abandoning their plans altogether.

The problem is therefore not simply a lack of execution.

It is the absence of a planning system that continuously helps users transform long-term goals into realistic, adaptive, and achievable daily actions.

## Origin Story

The idea for Daily Routine Helper originated from a simple but frustrating experience during my job search.

I once set a goal to apply for five jobs every day for one month. At first, the plan seemed reasonable: it was specific, measurable, and easy to track. However, it quickly became difficult to sustain.

Some days there were fewer than five positions that genuinely matched my background. On other days, even suitable opportunities required tailoring my resume before I could submit an application. As a result, I frequently failed to meet my daily target—not because I lacked effort, but because the plan itself failed to reflect reality.

Over time, I began to lose confidence in my ability to follow long-term plans. I questioned my discipline and even attributed the problem to my personality, believing that I simply was not someone who could stick to a structured plan.

Eventually, I realized that the problem was not my willingness to work.

The problem was that my plan was static, while reality was dynamic.

That realization fundamentally changed the way I think about planning.

A good plan should not only define a measurable goal—it should also adapt to changing circumstances, break long-term goals into realistic daily actions, and evolve based on what actually happened yesterday.

This realization became the starting point of Daily Routine Helper.

The product is built on a simple belief:

**A measurable plan is not necessarily an executable plan.**

The purpose of this project is not to encourage users to work harder. Instead, it aims to help them create plans that deserve to be followed—plans that are realistic, adaptive, and capable of building confidence through consistent daily progress.

# Root Cause

The challenges described above are not primarily caused by a lack of motivation or discipline. They stem from the way planning itself is typically designed.

### 1. Planning is disconnected from reality

Many plans are created under ideal assumptions rather than real-world conditions. Daily schedules, available opportunities, personal energy, and unexpected events constantly change, but traditional plans rarely adapt. As a result, even well-intentioned plans quickly become difficult to execute.

### 2. Planning does not learn from execution

Most planning systems treat each day as an independent event. They record what was completed but rarely use that information to improve future plans.

Effective planning should be adaptive. It should continuously learn from execution, reflection, and changing circumstances so that tomorrow's plan becomes more realistic than today's.

### 3. Users blame themselves instead of the planning system

When plans repeatedly fail, many people conclude that they lack discipline, consistency, or willpower.

In reality, the planning system itself is often the problem.

An unrealistic plan inevitably leads to frustration, reduced confidence, and eventually the abandonment of long-term goals.

Daily Routine Helper is built on the belief that planning should adapt to people—not the other way around.

The planning system should continuously learn from the user's execution patterns, while helping the user gradually build better planning habits.

A good planning system should learn from the user, just as the user learns from the planning system.

# Product Goals

Daily Routine Helper is designed to achieve the following long-term goals.

## 1. Transform long-term goals into achievable daily actions

Help users break down meaningful long-term goals into realistic, measurable, and actionable daily tasks.

The system should reduce the difficulty of planning by helping users answer a simple but important question:

**"What is the most meaningful thing I should do today?"**

---

## 2. Reduce the cognitive burden of planning

Planning itself can be mentally exhausting.

The product should minimize the effort required to create a realistic daily plan while still encouraging users to think intentionally about their priorities.

The goal is to reduce planning friction—not decision-making ability.

---

## 3. Continuously adapt through execution and reflection

Daily planning should never be static.

The planning system should continuously learn from the user's execution, reflection, and behavioral patterns to generate increasingly realistic plans over time.

Every day should improve the next day's planning.

---

## 4. Build confidence through sustainable progress

The objective is not to maximize daily productivity.

Instead, the product should help users experience consistent progress by creating plans that are realistic enough to complete and meaningful enough to move long-term goals forward.

Confidence is built through repeated success, not overwhelming ambition.

---

## 5. Help users become better planners

The ultimate goal of Daily Routine Helper is not to make users dependent on AI.

Instead, it should gradually teach users how effective planning works.

By making its reasoning transparent and continuously adapting to the user's behavior, the system should help users develop stronger planning skills, better self-awareness, and greater confidence in managing long-term goals independently.

A successful user should become better at planning because they have used Daily Routine Helper—not because they have become dependent on it.

# Core Principles

The following principles guide every product and design decision in Daily Routine Helper.

## 1. Sustainability over intensity

Daily planning should encourage consistent long-term progress rather than maximizing short-term productivity.

Plans should be realistic enough that users can complete most of them while maintaining motivation for the next day.

---

## 2. Every task must be measurable

Every planned task should have a clear definition of completion.

Avoid vague goals such as "work on resume."

Prefer concrete deliverables that users can easily evaluate.

---

## 3. Planning should adapt to reality

A plan should never be static.

It should continuously evolve based on execution, reflection, changing priorities, and real-world constraints.

---

## 4. Reflection is part of planning

Reflection is not the end of the planning process.

It is the starting point for tomorrow's plan.

Daily planning should form a continuous learning loop.

---

## 5. Explain before recommending

The system should not simply generate plans.

It should explain why each recommendation is made so that users gradually learn better planning strategies.

Transparent reasoning is an essential part of the user experience.

---

## 6. Teach planning instead of replacing it

The goal of the product is to help users become better planners over time.

AI should support independent thinking rather than creating long-term dependence.

Planning should be a collaboration between the user and the AI, not a command issued by the AI.

# User Workflow

Daily Routine Helper is designed around a simple daily workflow that minimizes planning effort while encouraging continuous improvement.

## Morning Planning

1. The user opens the application.
2. The system reviews the user's current state, including long-term goals, recent progress, and the previous day's reflection.
3. The AI generates today's planning strategy.
4. The AI proposes a daily plan consisting of realistic and measurable tasks.
5. The user reviews and confirms the plan before beginning the day.

The morning planning process should be lightweight and should normally take no more than a few minutes.

---

## Daily Execution

The user completes tasks throughout the day.

The application serves as a planning companion rather than a task enforcer.

Users are free to adjust or reorder tasks as reality changes.

---

## Daily Review

At the end of the day, the user reflects on the day's execution.

The review focuses on:

* What was completed
* What was not completed
* The biggest blocker
* The most important focus for tomorrow

The review should be simple enough to complete quickly, while still providing meaningful information for future planning.

---

## Continuous Improvement

The information collected during the daily review becomes part of the user's planning history.

It is used to generate a more realistic and personalized plan for the following day.

This creates a continuous feedback loop in which every day's execution improves the quality of future planning.

---

* Current progress

# Out of Scope

The MVP intentionally focuses on validating the core planning experience.

The following features are excluded from the first version, not because they are unimportant, but because they do not directly validate the core product hypothesis.

## The MVP will NOT include:

### Calendar Integration

Scheduling and calendar synchronization are valuable enhancements but are not required to validate adaptive daily planning.

---

### Apple Reminders / Third-party Export

Exporting tasks to external productivity tools is considered a future convenience feature rather than a core capability.

---

### Weekly and Monthly Reports

Long-term analytics will be introduced only after the daily planning workflow has been validated.

---

### Advanced Statistics and Dashboards

Visual analytics, productivity charts, streaks, and completion reports are intentionally postponed.

The priority is improving planning quality rather than measuring productivity.

---

### Gamification

Badges, points, levels, and reward systems are outside the scope of the MVP.

The product aims to build intrinsic motivation through sustainable progress rather than external rewards.

---

### Social Features

Sharing, collaboration, communities, and competitive features are not part of the initial release.

---

### Advanced AI Coaching

Deep coaching conversations, long-form reflective analysis, and personalized coaching sessions are future directions.

The MVP focuses on building a reliable daily planning loop.

---

## Product Principle

Features are excluded from the MVP not because they lack value, but because they do not directly contribute to validating the core hypothesis:

**Can adaptive daily planning help users consistently make meaningful progress toward long-term goals?**

Items listed in this section should be considered future possibilities rather than planned commitments. Their inclusion in future releases depends on whether they continue to support the product vision and solve validated user problems.
